\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{Workload and Candidate Statistics}

Let \(\Q\) be a finite workload of single-table conjunctive selection
queries.  Each \(q\in\Q\) has weight \(w_q>0\), true cardinality
\(N_q\geq0\), and target \(T_q=\max\{1,N_q\}\).  The floor defines a
logarithmic loss for zero-cardinality queries without flooring any raw
planner estimate.

Let \(\E\) be a finite candidate universe.  Candidate \(e\) has kind,
positive cost \(c_e\), and an attribute set \(A_e\) with \(|A_e|=2\);
two-column multivariate MCV statistics motivate the formulation.  The
Advisor chooses one unordered global creation set \(M\subseteq\E\) shared
by all queries, subject to
\[
  \sum_{e\in M}c_e\leq B,\qquad B\geq0.
\]
The budget is an upper bound, and no monotonicity of estimation accuracy is
assumed.  Creation, structural applicability, and actual estimator use are
distinct: creating \(e\) makes it available but neither makes it applicable
to every query nor forces the estimator to select it.

\subsection{Applicability and Conflicts}

For each query, let \(\E_q\subseteq\E\) denote the structurally
applicable candidates, determined by statistic kind, covered columns, and
supported predicate operators.  Applicability is not defined by a change
in a rounded singleton cardinality.  Since candidates have two attributes,
they form the undirected edge-labeled multigraph
\[
  H_q=(V_q,\E_q),\qquad V_q=\bigcup_{e\in\E_q}A_e.
\]
Each edge is the distinct candidate statistic whose endpoints are its two
attributes; candidates on the same attribute pair remain parallel edges.
Edges conflict when their endpoint sets intersect.  For a creation set \(M\),
the available query-local graph is
\[
  H_q[M]=(V_q,M\cap\E_q).
\]
Thus overlap is evaluated separately in each query graph, not by imposing a
single matching across the workload.

\subsection{Estimator Uncertainty}

A matching is a set of pairwise nonconflicting candidate edges.  We model
the complete compatible use schemes for \(q\) and \(M\) by
\[
  \Pmatch_q(M)=\operatorname{MaximalMatchings}(H_q[M]).
\]
Here maximal means that no available edge can be added; it does not mean
maximum cardinality.  The empty graph has the single maximal matching
\(\varnothing\).  Maximality abstracts greedy clause coverage: an
estimator continues selecting compatible applicable statistics until none
can be added, rather than arbitrarily selecting the empty set.

The numerical surrogate below applies to every pairwise compatible matching;
maximality is imposed only on the estimator-policy uncertainty set.  For a
considered policy \(\theta\), we require
\(P_q(M;\theta)\in\Pmatch_q(M)\); the policy may realize only a subset of
these matchings and may couple its choices across queries.

\subsection{Cardinality and Loss Models}

Let \(s_q(\varnothing)>0\) be the baseline pre-clamp selectivity and, for
each \(e\in\E_q\), let \(s_q(\{e\})>0\) be the corresponding singleton
selectivity in the same estimation context.  Define
\[
  r_{e,q}=\frac{s_q(\{e\})}{s_q(\varnothing)},\qquad
  b_{e,q}=\log r_{e,q}.
\]
The factors are positive, finite, and unrounded, never integer-like final
\texttt{Plan Rows}.  Conditional on the fixed-factor multiplicative
surrogate, every compatible matching \(P\) has
\[
  s_q(P)=s_q(\varnothing)\prod_{e\in P}r_{e,q}.
\]
Let \(R_q>0\) be the fixed relation-tuple multiplier across configurations.
Then
{\small
\[
  n_q(P)=R_qs_q(\varnothing)\prod_{e\in P}r_{e,q},\qquad
  \log n_q(P)=\log\bigl(R_qs_q(\varnothing)\bigr)
    +\sum_{e\in P}b_{e,q}.
\]
}
This multiplicativity is a conditional modeling assumption, not a PostgreSQL theorem.

For the ordinary finite positive regime, define the PostgreSQL row
transformation
\[
  C(x)=
  \begin{cases}
    1,&0<x\leq1,\\
    \operatorname{rint}(x),&1<x\leq x_{\max},
  \end{cases}
\]
where \(x_{\max}\) is the implementation's finite ordinary-regime cap and
\(\operatorname{rint}\) follows its floating-point rounding mode.  Inputs
outside this finite positive domain, including nonfinite values and any
implementation-specific very-large-value path, are outside this model's
scope.  The predicted final cardinality is \(\widehat N_q(P)=C(n_q(P))\):
raw factors are composed first, then \(C\) is applied, and only then is
log loss evaluated.

For \(P\in\Pmatch_q(M)\), define
\[
  \ell_q^{\mathrm{plan}}(P)=
  \left|\log C\!\left(R_qs_q(\varnothing)
    \prod_{e\in P}r_{e,q}\right)-\log T_q\right|.
\]

\subsection{Pessimistic Statistics Selection}

The \emph{Pessimistic Extended Statistics Selection} problem (PESS-ESS)
is
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
  \operatorname*{minimize}_{M\subseteq\E}\quad
    &\sum_{q\in\Q}w_q
      \max_{P\in\Pmatch_q(M)}\ell_q^{\mathrm{plan}}(P)\\
  \text{subject to}\quad
    &\sum_{e\in M}c_e\leq B.
\end{aligned}}
\]
The outer decision is global, while the inner maximum is query-local.

For a related raw-cardinality objective, let
\[
  \alpha_q=\log\frac{R_qs_q(\varnothing)}{T_q},\qquad
  \ell_q^{\mathrm{raw}}(P)=
    \left|\alpha_q+\sum_{e\in P}b_{e,q}\right|.
\]
Replacing plan loss by raw loss yields a distinct pessimistic objective;
rounding can make the two objectives select different creation sets.  A
secondary uniform-matching comparator is
\[
  F_U(M)=\sum_{q\in\Q}\frac{w_q}{|\Pmatch_q(M)|}
    \sum_{P\in\Pmatch_q(M)}\ell_q^{\mathrm{plan}}(P).
\]
It is uniform over legal maximal matchings, not over creation permutations,
and is not the primary PESS-ESS objective.  The formulation is therefore
conditional on single-table conjunctive queries, two-attribute candidates,
structural applicability, positive finite pre-clamp observations, fixed
relation multipliers, and the stated maximal-matching and multiplicative
assumptions.
