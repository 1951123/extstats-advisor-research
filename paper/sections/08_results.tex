\section{Results}

This section reports the validated evidence currently available for RQ1--RQ5.
The evidence-to-claim ledger is recorded in
\texttt{paper/evidence-to-manuscript-v1.json}; it distinguishes confirmatory
results, qualified causal comparisons, and experiments that remain missing.
Numbers below are derived from the committed summary artifacts and retain the
system-freeze and workload provenance of those artifacts.

\subsection{RQ1: effectiveness}
The validated RQ1a matched comparison is an in-workload result: the advisor
objective and evaluation use the same workload. It is therefore evidence of
workload-specific cardinality-estimation utility, not held-out generalization.
The table reports the immutable three-arm comparison; the RQ4 heuristic arms
are reported separately below.

\input{tables/rq1-cross-dataset-summary.tex}

The four-dataset summary is complete for the declared three-arm, in-workload
comparison under the system freeze recorded by those artifacts. It records
paired classifications, tail-error concentration, and the largest per-query
regressions in addition to the headline aggregates.
The v2 summary also separates observations-file provenance from the

These results do not claim that every query improves, do not replace the
PostgreSQL estimator with a learned model, and do not imply runtime speedups.

\subsection{RQ1b: held-out workload generalization}
Unlike RQ1a, which evaluates a recommendation on the workload used for
selection, RQ1b asks whether native extended statistics selected from a validation
workload retain cardinality-estimation utility on a held-out workload
generated under the same audited AreCEL workload-generator contract. This is a
same-distribution held-out evaluation, not a workload-drift or
distribution-shift experiment. For each dataset, the advisor used only
$W_{\mathrm{valid}}$ and authoritative validation truth to construct and seal
$S_{\mathrm{valid}}$. The sealed recommendation was then deployed to fresh
stock PostgreSQL, and each of the 10,000 test queries was evaluated once with
physical \texttt{EXPLAIN (FORMAT JSON)}. The comparison reuses the immutable
PG16-default and PG16-target10000 baseline arms; it does not rerun either
baseline. The design used 10,000 sampled rows with seed 42, statistics target
100, screening width $K_s=8$, at most $B=8$ selected definitions, and a
300-second search limit.

\input{tables/rq1b-held-out-generalization}

Table~\ref{tab:rq1b-held-out-generalization} shows that $S_{\mathrm{valid}}$
has lower mean held-out q-error than both historical baseline arms on all four
datasets. The reduction versus PG16-default is 57.3\% for Census13, 6.5\%
for Forest10, 49.5\% for Power7, and 85.4\% for DMV11. Thus the direction is
consistent, but the magnitude is strongly dataset-dependent; the DMV11 value
is not a universal improvement rate. All four searches terminated at the
recorded local optimum, which does not establish global optimality.

The mean reductions are not uniform per-query improvements. Relative to
PG16-default, the paired counts (improved/tied/worsened) are 4,936/2,909/2,155
for Census13, 4,322/2,811/2,867 for Forest10, 5,719/575/3,706 for Power7, and
6,518/1,027/2,455 for DMV11. Consequently, 21.6\%--37.1\% of test instances
worsened despite the lower mean. The p95 and p99 q-errors decrease on all four
datasets, consistent descriptively with fewer severe errors. Maximum q-error
also decreases for Census13, Forest10, and DMV11, but increases slightly for
Power7 (153,148 to 155,314); these tails do not support a causal explanation.

The preregistered strict-unseen analysis filters the same physical test
observations and is secondary, not a second experiment. It removes 614
Census13 instances and 1,862 DMV11 instances whose exact source-query hashes
were seen in validation, leaving 9,386 and 8,138 instances respectively. The
mean reductions versus PG16-default remain positive: 57.95\% for Census13,
84.51\% for DMV11, 6.55\% for Forest10, and 49.52\% for Power7. Forest10 and
Power7 have strict-unseen populations equal to the full test population, so
they provide no within-dataset contrast between those populations. Strict
unseen means exact source-query non-overlap, not a different query
distribution.

The identifier-level intersection between $S_{\mathrm{valid}}$ and the
historical in-workload $S_{\mathrm{test}}$ is zero for all four datasets. The
published evidence does not establish that candidate IDs from independently
constructed snapshots denote comparable physical definitions, so this is not
evidence of disjoint PostgreSQL statistics. In particular, $S_{\mathrm{test}}$
is neither an oracle nor an optimality bound. Census13 also retains its
audited historical baseline caveat: the RQ1a baseline was truth-rebound from
historical evidence, its PG16-default arm inherited target $-1$, and its
PG16-target10000 arm used an explicit target of 10,000; RQ1b selection used
target 100.

\subsection{Ordering sensitivity ablation}
The fixed-membership ordering ablation asks a narrower systems question than
RQ1b: with the selected native statistics held fixed, does changing their
activation order change PostgreSQL's cardinality estimates? For each of the
four datasets, we replayed the published $S_{\mathrm{valid}}$ membership in
its reference order, the exact reverse order, and five deterministic random
orders (seeds 17, 29, 43, 71, and 101). The hypothetical arm reused one
native payload realization, ordinary-statistics fingerprint, workload, truth
mapping, and planner configuration per dataset. It therefore isolates
activation order within the registered patched-PostgreSQL what-if contract;
it does not rerun Advisor selection or access $W_{\mathrm{test}}$.

\input{tables/oid-order-sensitivity-ablation}

The controlled hypothetical observations show order sensitivity, but with
substantial dataset heterogeneity. Reversing the reference order increased
mean q-error from 2.279 to 3.361 on Census13, from 6.468 to 6.659 on
Forest10, from 48.336 to 75.619 on Power7, and from 48.964 to 54.193 on
DMV11. The reference order ranked first among the seven declared treatments
on the first three datasets and tied the best treatment on DMV11, where
random seed 17 produced identical observations. These are descriptive
comparisons over five preregistered alternatives, not evidence that the
Advisor order is globally optimal.

The paired changes are not uniform: relative to the reference, alternative
hypothetical orders changed Plan Rows for 13.7--26.2\% of Census13 queries,
18.7--26.4\% of Forest10 queries, 11.0--42.6\% of Power7 queries, and
0--30.7\% of DMV11 queries. Mean differences can therefore reflect a
minority of queries, including tail changes, rather than a uniform shift in
all estimates. The synthetic overlapping-MCV witness gives a mechanism-level
diagnostic: the A--B and B--A orders produced root Plan Rows 303 and 281,
respectively, versus 221 with no extended statistics, while all five matched
physical/hypothetical witness arms agreed exactly. The PostgreSQL source
supports this bounded interpretation: tied MCV candidates are selected by
the order of the relation statistics list, and the patched overlay supplies
the registered order. The witness does not establish a universal OID rule
for every statistics kind or workload.

The physical-stock arm achieved the prescribed OID orders, but every
non-reference comparison was classified as
\texttt{confounded-operational-deployment}: native payload and
ordinary-statistics fingerprints changed across independent \texttt{ANALYZE}
realizations. Those observations are retained as operational evidence, but
are not combined with the hypothetical arm and are not interpreted as pure
OID-order causal effects. Neither arm supports a claim about query-runtime
improvement.

\subsection{RQ2: sample-to-full-data transfer}
RQ2a compares three full-data physical states. $P_0$ is a fresh baseline,
$P_1$ deploys the immutable recommendation and runs one native full-data
\texttt{ANALYZE}, and $P_2$ removes only advisor-managed objects from the same
realization without another \texttt{ANALYZE}. The $P_2$--$P_1$ comparison is
the controlled transfer comparison; $P_0$--$P_1$ also includes the ordinary-
statistics refresh caused by deployment. The four formal children and their
cross-dataset summary are complete.

\input{tables/rq2-sample-full-transfer}

Table~\ref{tab:rq2-sample-full-transfer} shows lower $P_1$ q-error than both
comparison states on all four datasets. The controlled reduction ranges from
7.1\% on Forest10 to 83.1\% on DMV11. Query-level regressions remain. The
$P_0$--$P_1$ improved/unchanged/worsened counts are 5,183/2,917/1,900
(Census13), 4,415/2,814/2,771 (Forest10), 4,016/575/5,409 (Power7), and
6,881/1,013/2,106 (DMV11). Thus aggregate
transfer benefit does not imply uniform query improvement.

RQ2b compares the sample-side objective $J_S(M)$ with the full-data objective
$J_D(M;A)$ and records the complete 3$\times$3 direction table per dataset.
The descriptive Spearman values range from 0.478 (Power7) to 0.968
(Census13), while opposite-direction query fractions range from 0.64\% to
3.17\%. The sample-side reduction is positive for every dataset, but its size
does not determine the full-data reduction: independent native realization,
ordinary-statistics refresh, and the two objectives are distinct. These are
transfer relationships, not mechanism-fidelity measurements.

\subsection{RQ3: fidelity}
RQ3 tests mechanism fidelity rather than sample/full transfer. The primary
same-patched-binary comparison controls schema, data, workload, ordinary
statistics, payload correspondence, planner settings, and statistics target.
The secondary comparison uses stock and patched physical realizations with the
overlay inactive. Both formal artifacts report exact root \texttt{Plan Rows}
agreement for all nine pairs.

The result supports the registered synthetic clause forms and controls only.
It does not establish fidelity for arbitrary SQL, data distributions,

same-binary fidelity evidence.

\subsection{RQ4: ablation and advisor necessity}
RQ4a is the deterministic patched-sandbox method comparison at a fixed
$k=4$; RQ4b is the separate stock physical consequence study under one shared
native realization. The four-dataset fixed-$k$ evidence is complete at
dataset scope, with Forest10 retaining its historical v1 provenance rather
than being silently reassigned to v2.

\input{tables/rq4-fixed-k}

Greedy ADD is best among the recorded methods on Forest10, Power7, and DMV11,
but singleton utility is better on Census13. This counterexample is important:
the result supports a workload- and dataset-dependent benefit of planner-in-
the-loop search, not universal superiority. Census13 and DMV11 Greedy traces
are budget-censored feasible incumbents retained after incomplete final rounds;
their comparisons do not imply exhaustive optimality. Power7 reaches its
maximum-statistics-count termination before the 300-second search deadline.
Forest10 is historical v1 evidence with a 3,600-second declared search
budget, rather than a fourth observation under the current 300-second
deadline. RQ4b retains the physical observations and common-realization
controls separately from the deterministic RQ4a objectives.

The validated $K_s$ sensitivity campaign covers Census13, Power7, and DMV11
at $K_s\in\{4,8,16,32,\mathrm{all}\}$ with $B=4$.

\input{tables/rq4-ks-sensitivity}

The best finite screening width is 8, 16, and 32 for Census13, Power7, and
DMV11 respectively. The $\mathrm{all}$ point is budget-censored for Census13
and DMV11; it is a bounded incumbent rather than an optimum reference. The
fixed-evaluation-budget comparison was cancelled by research-team decision
after the offline time-budget fairness audit; its historical protocol remains
preserved, but no results were generated. The subsequent Native
\texttt{ANALYZE} Stability v1 campaign
completed its declared raw observation matrix but failed its original
nonempty-payload eligibility gate: six selected dependency payloads were
recorded as empty. PostgreSQL's frozen source permits SQL NULL dependency
data when no nonzero dependency is retained, but the v1 capture did not
preserve an independent SQL-NULL marker. We therefore retain the campaign's
original failed/ineligible status and report only a qualified, post-hoc v2
analysis.

\input{tables/native-analyze-stability-v2}

Table~\ref{tab:native-analyze-stability-v2} summarizes the descriptive
stability result. Within every realization, Greedy ADD ranked first on
Forest10 and DMV11, whereas singleton utility ranked first on Census13;
there were no winner reversals across the five realizations. Greedy's mean
q-error was lower than singleton utility in all five Forest10 and DMV11
realizations, but higher in all five Census13 realizations. Ordinary-statistics
fingerprints changed between the first and each later realization for all
three datasets, and extended-statistics fingerprints also varied. Thus the
analysis measures combined native-realization variability, not an
extended-statistics-only causal component. Five realizations provide
descriptive evidence rather than a probability guarantee, and Forest10's
historical-v1 provenance remains a separate stratum.

\subsection{RQ5: practicality}
RQ5 is partially complete. The validated snapshot-footprint and static-
deployment subexperiments, together with the versioned trace inventory,
provide stage-specific measurements but not a single end-to-end production
cost.

\input{tables/rq5-practicality}

The direct measurements separate snapshot capture and logical footprint from
stock DDL, native \texttt{ANALYZE}, catalog bytes, and planner-time change.
The four-dataset median sealed snapshot sizes are 3.56--5.69 MiB;
static DDL is approximately 0.0023--0.0027 seconds, whereas relation-level
\texttt{ANALYZE} is 0.274--0.391 seconds. The trace inventory also measures
authoritative external truth import (approximately 0.14--0.16 seconds),
singleton profiling, search, and full-workload planning stages. Logical and
physical storage are not additive, and the physical allocation values are
page-granular rather than exact per-object attribution. Production-exact
truth collection, refresh quality trends, and a canonical end-to-end advisor
cost remain unmeasured. The absence of a learned-model inference path is not
treated as zero operational overhead.

\subsection{Evidence status and remaining studies}

The results above distinguish validated evidence from bounded or incomplete
studies. RQ1a, RQ1b, RQ2a/RQ2b, and the two RQ3 fidelity checks have validated
four-dataset or registered-fixture evidence. RQ4 is complete only for the
reported fixed-$k$ and $K_s$ dataset-scope studies: the historical
fixed-evaluation-budget comparison is cancelled with no results, while native
\texttt{ANALYZE} stability is preregistered but not executed.
RQ5 is a qualified partial practicality account: the measured stages are
reported above, while production-exact truth timing, refresh quality trends,
and a canonical end-to-end cost remain open. Historical pilot outputs remain
immutable and are not silently promoted to confirmatory evidence or reassigned
to a different system freeze.
