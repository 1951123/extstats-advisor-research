\section{Discussion and Limitations}

This study has several boundaries. First, the current benchmark lineage and
truth-collection implementation are primarily single-table and
simple-selection. Production exact truth is not a contract for arbitrary
production SQL: the v1 collector fails closed outside its supported sealed
one-relation, AND-predicate, row-preserving query shape. The architecture does
not establish behavior for joins, partitioned inheritance, or all
expression-statistics cases. Second, the what-if substrate is PostgreSQL-
specific and currently supports only the native MCV and functional-dependency
payload paths described in Section~5.

Third, not every query improves. Benefits depend on workload predicates,
statistics target, sample representativeness, and the planner's native
combination rules. A sample-selected design can become stale under data or
workload drift, and the current search provides no guarantee of a globally
optimal configuration. Candidate limits and search budgets are therefore
experimental variables, not universal defaults.

The ordering ablation adds a narrower qualification to this interpretation.
Under the controlled hypothetical realization, fixed membership and changed
activation order alter estimates for a dataset-dependent subset of queries;
the Advisor reference order is favorable within the seven preregistered
orders, but this finite comparison is not an optimality result. The
overlapping-MCV witness is consistent with the PostgreSQL implementation's
tie behavior, while remaining a single patched-PostgreSQL fixture rather than
a universal mechanism claim. The physical deployment comparisons are
operationally informative but confounded by native payload and ordinary
statistics drift after independent \texttt{ANALYZE} runs, so they cannot be
attributed to OID order alone.

The transfer results provide a complementary boundary. Across the four
datasets, the deployed sample-selected recommendation improved the controlled
full-data comparison, but the magnitude varied and paired regressions
remained. Sample-side utility was descriptively associated with full-data
utility, with Spearman values ranging from 0.478 to 0.968; this is not a
guarantee because native full-data statistics are independently realized and
the objectives are distinct. The RQ3 fidelity checks are narrower still: exact
Plan Rows agreement was established for the registered synthetic clause forms,
not for arbitrary workloads or PostgreSQL versions.

RQ4 shows why the search claim must remain conditional. Greedy ADD was the
best recorded fixed-$k$ method on three datasets, while singleton utility was
better on Census13. The screening-width results also include budget-censored
incumbents, and neither fixed-evaluation-budget nor native-\texttt{ANALYZE}
stability has been measured. These results support dataset- and
workload-dependent ablation findings rather than universal superiority.
The practicality measurements similarly cover selected stages---snapshot
footprint, deployment DDL, \texttt{ANALYZE}, and planner-time traces---rather
than a complete production cost model.

For the completed RQ1b analysis, the evidence is narrower than a broad
generalization claim. It covers only four audited AreCEL datasets whose
validation and test workloads follow the same generator contract. Strict-unseen
removes exact source-query overlap, but not all structural similarity. The
reported outcome is planner cardinality-estimation q-error rather than query
execution latency, and paired regressions remain on every dataset. Improvement
magnitude is heterogeneous. Candidate IDs from independent advisor runs are
not established as physical-definition identifiers, so zero identifier overlap
does not prove disjoint PostgreSQL definitions. Census13 additionally retains
its documented historical baseline provenance and statistics-policy caveat.

Fourth, truth acquisition itself has operational cost. Authoritative external
labels require import and binding validation, but they are not evidence that
the advisor executed the labels itself. Production exact truth can require
expensive exact-count queries even in a read-only repeatable-read source view.
The absence of a learned-model training/inference path does not
make sample capture, truth acquisition, native construction, planner search,
or deployment \texttt{ANALYZE} free.

Fifth, literature comparisons with learned cardinality estimators are not
necessarily apples-to-apples: systems, workload generation, data snapshots,
truth production, and metric definitions can differ. The Are We Ready results
are useful context and benchmark lineage, not a substitute for matched
experiments.

Sixth, production-compatible deployment does not by itself establish
production readiness. The add-only contract deliberately leaves arbitrary
existing extended statistics outside advisor ownership. The DBA must decide
whether such objects remain, are removed, are replaced, or coexist. The
optimizer evaluated $M^*$, not necessarily
$E_{\mathrm{existing}}\cup M^*$, so the SearchResult objective is not a
guarantee for the combined state.

Finally, the final \texttt{ANALYZE} needed by a non-empty recommendation can
rebuild ordinary statistics and externally managed extended-statistics payloads
on the same relation. That is an operational effect to measure and disclose,
not evidence that the advisor owns those external objects. These limitations
motivate the fidelity, practicality, drift, and reconciliation follow-up work
listed in the experiment plan.
