\section{Conclusion}

This paper frames \advisor around a deliberately conservative question: before
replacing PostgreSQL's cardinality estimator, how far can the native statistics
already consumed by that estimator take us? The proposed answer is evaluated
through an isolated planner-in-the-loop what-if substrate, a fixed-sample
design workflow, a bounded transparent search, and a stock PostgreSQL
deployment recommendation.

The framework makes the evidence boundaries explicit. The current artifacts
are pilot evidence; confirmatory conclusions require immutable provenance for
each RQ. Existing production extended statistics remain outside advisor
ownership, and a SearchResult objective is not a guarantee for a combined
catalog that the optimizer did not evaluate. The next step is therefore not to
broaden the claim, but to complete the RQ1--RQ5 protocol, audit fidelity and
cost, and report where native statistics help, where they do not, and what
operational trade-off they impose.
