\section{Conclusion}

This paper frames \advisor around a deliberately conservative question: before
replacing PostgreSQL's cardinality estimator, how far can the native statistics
already consumed by that estimator take us? The proposed answer is evaluated
through an isolated planner-in-the-loop what-if substrate, a fixed-sample
design workflow, a bounded transparent search, and a stock PostgreSQL
deployment recommendation.

The framework makes the evidence boundaries explicit. The four-dataset RQ1b
results provide validated held-out evidence that validation-selected native
statistics can reduce mean cardinality-estimation q-error under the audited
AreCEL generator contract, with substantial dataset heterogeneity and
per-query regressions. This does not establish workload-drift robustness,
execution-time improvement, or global optimality. Existing production extended
statistics remain outside advisor ownership, and a SearchResult objective is
not a guarantee for a combined catalog that the optimizer did not evaluate.
The completed RQ2 transfer evidence shows that sample-selected designs can
retain utility after independent full-data native-statistics realization, but
does not guarantee the sample-side objective or eliminate query-level
regressions. RQ3 establishes fidelity only for its registered synthetic
fixture. RQ4 provides qualified fixed-$k$ and screening-width ablations. The
historical fixed-evaluation-budget study was cancelled with no results, and
the Native \texttt{ANALYZE} Stability v1 campaign is failed/ineligible with
evidence preserved; its v2 analysis is a qualified post-hoc descriptive
reanalysis. RQ5 provides stage-specific practicality measurements rather than
a complete end-to-end production cost. The ordering ablation likewise supports
only the bounded hypothetical and confounded physical claims reported in the
Results section. These evidence gaps and limitations remain explicit rather
than being hidden by a single aggregate conclusion.
