\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents \advisor as a way to explore database-native statistical
design without replacing the deployed cardinality estimator. The system builds
native MCV and dependency payloads once on a fixed typed sample, registers them
in a backend-local PostgreSQL what-if overlay, and varies ordered
configurations through the existing planner's native selectivity paths. A
workload-aware pipeline then profiles candidates, performs bounded Greedy ADD
search, and emits an ordered Recommendation that a DBA can deploy with stock
PostgreSQL.

The evidence shows substantial but dataset-dependent q-error improvement,
qualified same-generator held-out and sample-to-full transfer, registered
mechanism fidelity, and a bounded ordering effect. It also preserves the
counterexample where Singleton utility beats Greedy ADD, physical-order
confounding, the failed/ineligible Native \texttt{ANALYZE} v1 campaign and
post-hoc v2 analysis, and the incomplete production cost account. The
contribution is therefore not global optimization or production universality;
it is a database-native what-if separation that makes workload-aware
statistics design and its limitations measurable.
