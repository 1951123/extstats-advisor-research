\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Cardinality estimation errors remain a source of poor query plans, while replacing a database system's estimator introduces operational, maintenance, and trust costs. This paper presents \advisor, a workload-aware design system for PostgreSQL native extended statistics. Its catalogless what-if mechanism materializes native MCV and dependency payloads once on a fixed typed sample, registers them in backend-local state, and activates ordered configurations through PostgreSQL's native planner paths. The Advisor derives workload-relevant candidates, profiles singleton utility against independently bound truth, runs bounded Greedy ADD search, and emits an ordered Recommendation deployable through stock PostgreSQL \texttt{CREATE STATISTICS} and \texttt{ANALYZE}. We evaluate effectiveness, same-generator held-out behavior, sample-to-full-data transfer, registered mechanism fidelity, heuristic comparisons, practicality, bounded order sensitivity, and qualified native-realization variability across an audited single-table benchmark lineage. The evidence includes validated four-dataset RQ1b and transfer results, but also preserves dataset heterogeneity, the Census13 heuristic counterexample, physical-order confounding, and the post-hoc status of Native \texttt{ANALYZE} Stability. We do not claim a replacement cardinality estimator, global optimality, or complete production-cost validation.
\end{abstract}
